Colorimetry Training

7 short slides, swipe or tap through. It's here so the numbers in the Delta E Calculator mean something. Forgot a term? Check the glossary.

1. Why Lab exists

  • Two people can look at the same panel and disagree how far off it is — measurement gives everyone the same number to argue from.
  • Plain RGB isn't perceptually uniform: the same numeric step can look like a bigger or smaller difference depending on where it sits in the space.
  • CIELAB (1976) was built so equal numeric distances ≈ equal perceived differences — that's what makes "ΔE = 1.2" mean the same thing across completely different colours.
L* = lightness (0 black → 100 white) · a* = red↔green · b* = yellow↔blue.

2. The axes: L*, a*, b*, C*, h

  • C* (chroma) = distance from neutral grey = √(a*² + b*²) — how vivid the colour is.
  • h (hue angle) = atan2(b*, a*), 0°–360° — 0° red-ish, 90° yellow, 180° green, 270° blue.

3. Illuminants & observer

  • Colour = light source + object + eye. Change the light, the same panel can measure differently.
  • Illuminant = standardized light (D65 daylight, A incandescent, F11 fluorescent). Observer = 2°/10° field of view behind the colour-matching functions.
  • Metamerism: two panels match under one light, clash under another — the most common cause of "it matched in the booth, not at the loading dock".

4. The instrument

  • 45/0 (or 0/45): light at 45°, reads straight on — default for solid colours.
  • Sphere (d/8): integrates light from every direction — used when gloss shouldn't influence the reading.
  • Multiangle (15°/45°/110°): required for metallics/pearls — the colour genuinely changes with viewing angle.
  • Always: calibrate on the white standard, and let the panel reach the state you'll judge it in before measuring.

45/0 geometry — the common default for solid colours

5. Why four Delta E formulas

  • CIE76: straight-line distance — oldest, overstates differences in saturated colours.
  • CIE94: stretches the tolerance based on chroma.
  • CMC l:c: weights lightness vs. chroma independently (2:1 = acceptability default, 1:1 = stricter).
  • CIEDE2000: current best practice — also corrects a known hue-rotation issue in blue tones.

Same numeric tolerance, same colour — four different tolerance shapes

Agree the formula + tolerance with your customer upfront — a batch can pass on one and fail on another, and that's not a bug in either number.

6. Reading the difference

  • ΔE tells you how much — it says nothing about which way to correct.
  • ΔL*/ΔC*/ΔH* (or ΔL*/Δa*/Δb*) give the direction: darker/lighter, more/less saturated, and which hue family the shift leans toward.
  • +b* is yellow — a positive Δb* means the sample went yellower, so the correction direction is toward blue.

7. Gloss & appearance

  • Higher gloss → more specular reflection → tends to read darker and more saturated, even at identical Lab values.
  • A ΔE of 0.8 can look wrong on the shop floor if the gloss units (GU) drifted enough.
  • A master record should carry a gloss value and tolerance alongside L*a*b* — not just the colour.
